# IA-MNS Identity

[ADR-0022](../adr/0022-own-the-ia-mns-identity-with-verified-external-links.md) · [ADR-0023](../adr/0023-serve-one-frontend-to-three-surfaces-with-host-identity-proofs.md) · [ADR-0025](../adr/0025-administer-the-authentication-policy-within-fixed-safeguards.md) · [ADR-0026](../adr/0026-host-ia-mns-in-sankhya-om-through-an-in-repository-add-on.md) · [Corporate agent](corporate-agent.md) · [Setup](../setup.md#identity) · [Human actions](../project/human-actions.md)

## Model

The `identity` API module (`apps/api/src/features/identity/`) owns who a person is in IA-MNS and what they may do. The web module (`apps/web/src/features/identity/`) owns sign-in, first access, account and administration screens and the embedded host bridge. Host components only frame the web application and supply proofs: the PDT host page belongs to PDT Connect, and the Sankhya Om add-on is [`apps/sankhya-addon`](../../apps/sankhya-addon/README.md).

| Concept         | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Person          | The single principal. Its UUID is the access-token `orion_principal_id` and the owner of conversations. Disabled or `merged` into another Person, never hard-deleted.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Local account   | Optional login and scrypt password, temporary lockout, optional TOTP second factor with ten single-use recovery codes.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| External link   | A verified `(provider, issuer, subject)`: PDT `identitySubject` under `PDT_IDENTITY_ISSUER`, or Sankhya `CODUSU` under `SANKHYA_IDENTITY_ISSUER`. One account maps to one Person; one account per provider installation per Person. `established_by` is `proof` (the person proved the account) or `directory` (an owner selected the real Sankhya user). The provider-reported e-mail is kept only as a duplicate hint. |
| Session         | Method (`local`, `pdt`, `sankhya`, `bootstrap`, `enrollment`), surface (`direct`, `pdt`, `sankhya`), assurance (`single`, `mfa`), authentication time, idle and absolute expiry.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Role            | `owner`: principal administrator, independent of Sankhya and PDT roles.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| WhatsApp number | Optional number an owner linked to the Person: the only authorization of the [WhatsApp channel](whatsapp-channel.md). An identity attribute, never a sign-in method; see [WhatsApp numbers](identity.md#whatsapp-numbers).                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Grant           | An explicit owner grant of a registered catalog permission to a Person or a group, optionally restricted to ERP records; see [Authorization](identity.md#authorization).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Group           | An owner-managed set of Persons; every member holds the group's grants.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

Physical structure, constraints and classification are in the [schema reference](../generated/database/schema.md) (`identity_*` tables). Names and e-mails are never identity keys. A Person may hold any combination of a local account, a Sankhya link and a PDT link; all of them reach the same history, preferences and permissions.

## Sign-in methods and surfaces

| Surface                    | How a Person signs in                                                                                                      | Session                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Direct (`/`)               | IA-MNS login and password (+TOTP), "Entrar com PDT Connect" (contract redirect), "Entrar com Sankhya" (Om add-on redirect) | Access token in memory; rotating refresh credential in an HttpOnly cookie (`__Host-ia-mns-session`, SameSite=Strict) whose duration and inactivity timeout follow the [authentication policy](#authentication-policy) (defaults 12 h and 2 h) |
| PDT (`/embed/pdt`)         | Automatic: the PDT host page supplies a PDT authorization code for the pending flow                                        | Memory only; 10-minute session renewed by a new handshake; no cookies                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Sankhya (`/embed/sankhya`) | Automatic: the Om add-on supplies a signed assertion for the pending flow                                                  | Same as PDT                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

On the direct surface every product screen requires a signed-in person; anyone else is sent to `/entrar`. The surface is audit metadata; capabilities never depend on it. The same Person, conversations, links and permissions are used everywhere.

### Direct provider flows

1. `POST /identity/providers/{provider}/start` with `mode: "direct"` creates a 5-minute single-use flow (state, nonce, PKCE verifier) and sets a browser-binding cookie: `ia-mns-pdt` with SameSite=Lax (PDT returns by top-level GET) or `ia-mns-sankhya` with SameSite=None (the Om returns by cross-site form post). Over HTTPS both carry `__Host-` and `Secure`.
2. PDT: the browser goes to the contract `/integrations/authorize`; PDT redirects to `PDT_IDENTITY_REDIRECT_URI` (`GET /identity/pdt/callback`). Sankhya: the browser goes to `SANKHYA_IDENTITY_AUTHORIZE_URL?state&nonce`; the add-on form-posts `assertion` and `state` to `POST /identity/sankhya/callback`.
3. The API checks the state ticket and the binding cookie, redeems the PDT code server-side (client secret, PKCE, identity lookup, immediate revoke) or verifies the assertion, then redirects to `/`, `/conta?novo=1` (profile created at first access), `/entrar/primeiro-acesso` (first access waiting for confirmation, or a login started from it with `resumeFirstAccess`), `/conta?vinculado=<provider>` (link or link invitation), `/conta?unificar=<provider>` (consolidation offer), `/conta?confirmado=1` (reauthentication) or `/entrar?erro=CODE`. Callback queries are never logged and responses use `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`.

### Embedded handshake (bridge protocol v1)

1. The frame calls `POST /identity/providers/{provider}/start` with `mode: "embedded"`. It receives a secret `pendingId` (kept in memory instead of a cookie), the configured host origin and, for PDT, `state` and `codeChallenge` (S256), or, for Sankhya, a `nonce`.
2. The frame posts to `window.parent` with target origin = host origin:
   - PDT: `{ v: 1, type: "ia-mns:auth-request", requestId, provider: "pdt", state, code_challenge }`
   - Sankhya: `{ v: 1, type: "ia-mns:auth-request", requestId, provider: "sankhya", nonce }`
3. The host answers only its own IA-MNS iframe, with target origin = IA-MNS origin:
   - PDT: `{ v: 1, type: "ia-mns:auth-response", requestId, code, state, iss }`
   - Sankhya: `{ v: 1, type: "ia-mns:auth-response", requestId, assertion }`
   - Failure: `{ v: 1, type: "ia-mns:auth-error", requestId, error }` (for example `pdt_login_required`, `no_authenticated_user`).
4. The frame sends the proof to `POST /identity/providers/{provider}/complete`; the API verifies it and returns an access token (with `provisioned: true` on an automatic first access), a confirmation request, or a public error.

PDT Connect also sets the display theme: it opens the frame with `?theme=light|dark` and sends `{ v: 1, type: "ia-mns:host-theme", theme }` on load and whenever the person changes the PDT theme. Inside PDT the frame follows that theme, hides its own theme control and stores nothing, so the direct-URL preference is unaffected; a theme message is applied only once the handshake has confirmed the PDT origin. The Sankhya Om sends no theme, and the frame keeps its own light default there.

The frame accepts messages only from the configured host origin and its parent window. These checks are defense in depth: an intercepted code is useless without the client secret and PKCE verifier held by the API, and an assertion is bound to the server nonce and accepted once.

A refused or failed handshake shows its message once, with **Tentar novamente**; nothing retries automatically. Ending an embedded session discards cached person data but keeps the public identity status, so it never starts another handshake by itself.

### PDT host component (outside this repository)

The PDT Connect repository (not this one) provides the **IA-MNS** screen (route `/ia-mns`, permission `ia-mns:acessar`), which frames `/embed/pdt` with `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"` and implements step 3 for PDT. It reads its fixed configuration from the PDT parameters `IA_MNS_URL`, `IA_MNS_CLIENT_ID` and `IA_MNS_REDIRECT_URI` through `GET /api/ia-mns/host`, which refuses values outside `PDT_IDENTITY_CLIENTS`, and calls the **existing** contract v1 endpoint `POST /api/auth/integrations/authorize` with the signed-in PDT JWT and that `client_id` and `redirect_uri` (never values sent by the frame). It forwards only the `code`, `state` and `iss` of the returned `redirectUrl`, without navigating. An invalid PDT session answers `pdt_login_required`. The PDT guide `guides/integration-identity.md` documents the screen; client registration, parameters and deployment are PDT operations ([PH-12](../project/human-actions.md#ph-12)).

The embedded flow never visits the registered callback; it only binds the code to the client. IA-MNS therefore offers "Entrar com PDT Connect" on its direct URL only when `PDT_IDENTITY_REDIRECT_URI` is on `IA_MNS_PUBLIC_ORIGIN`, where the direct flow's binding cookie lives; an installation may register a callback elsewhere for embedded use only.

### Sankhya Om add-on assertion contract

The add-on ([`apps/sankhya-addon`](../../apps/sankhya-addon/README.md), [ADR-0026](../adr/0026-host-ia-mns-in-sankhya-om-through-an-in-repository-add-on.md)) runs inside the Om. Its menu screen frames `/embed/sankhya` and answers step 3 for Sankhya; it renders no IA-MNS screen and keeps no IA-MNS state or business rule. It must:

- read the authenticated user from the **server-side** session (never from the browser) and refuse requests that did not arrive through the configured Om HTTPS name. On 2026-10-06 the owner decided that CODUSU 0 (the shared SUP account) is accepted, so it can be consolidated into an administrator's Person; anyone who signs in to the Om as SUP then acts as that Person, and the audit cannot tell individuals apart;
- mint a compact JWS with header `{ alg: "RS256" | "ES256", typ: "JWT", kid }` and claims `iss` = `SANKHYA_IDENTITY_ISSUER`, `aud` = `IA_MNS_IDENTITY_AUDIENCE`, `sub` = CODUSU (decimal string), `nonce` = the flow nonce, `jti` = unique id, `iat`, and `exp` ≤ `iat` + 120 seconds; the `name` and `email` (the user's TSIUSU e-mail, used only as a duplicate hint) claims are optional;
- expose the mint operation only to same-origin POST requests from its own embedding page (custom header, `Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin`), and the direct-URL page only as a top-level document that form-posts to the fixed IA-MNS callback;
- keep the private key outside sources editable by ordinary component authors, publishing only public keys (`SANKHYA_IDENTITY_KEYS`, a JWKS that IA-MNS pins).

The add-on implements this contract for the embedded surface: its `IaMnsHostSP` service requires the custom header `X-IA-MNS-Host` and the configured Om origin, signs ES256 assertions valid for 60 seconds with a key kept in the WildFly configuration directory, and its screen frames IA-MNS with `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"` ([add-on guide](../../apps/sankhya-addon/README.md#how-it-works)). On 2026-10-06 it was validated in the Sankhya development Om with a real signed-in user ([PJ-24](../project/implementation-plan.md#current-work)); the direct-URL authorize page is not implemented yet. In production only the Om HTTPS name is trusted (owner decision of 2026-10-06): when the Om is opened through another address, such as a plain-HTTP address, the screen does not frame IA-MNS and tells the person to open the Om through the secure address. Trusting a production Om session awaits [PH-11](../project/human-actions.md#ph-11), and production refuses this connector until `SANKHYA_SESSION_TRUST=approved`.

## First access, duplicates and linking

There is no public account creation. People who use Sankhya or PDT Connect need no prior registration; local credentials come only from the bootstrap, owner invitations, or an authenticated Person adding a password as another method.

- **Automatic first access.** A verified PDT or Sankhya account that is not linked creates its Person and link atomically (`person.provisioned` audit, `provisioned: true`), named from the provider-reported name. Concurrent first accesses of one account converge on one Person through the link's unique key.
- **When it stops instead.** Creation pauses with `provision_required` and a 10-minute single-use ticket (response body when embedded, HttpOnly cookie when direct) when:
  - `reason: "candidate"`: the account reports an e-mail equal to the one recorded on another provider's link of an active Person that does not already have an account of this provider. `methods` lists that Person's sign-in methods available here. The person either proves the profile (signs in with one of them, then `POST /identity/provision/link` attaches the account) or states it is not theirs (`POST /identity/provision/create` creates a separate Person). The e-mail alone never links anything.
  - `reason: "signed_in"` (direct only): the browser already holds a session of a Person when the login started. The person attaches the account to that Person or creates a separate one.
    `POST /identity/provision/inspect` describes a pending ticket without consuming it.
- **Linking later** (`intent: "link"`) requires a current session with recent authentication (policy window, default 10 minutes) and a new proof. If the account already belongs to a Person whose only method is that account (no password, grants, group memberships or owner role), the result is `merge_available`; confirming with `POST /identity/merge` (same session) consolidates that Person into the current one. Any other existing owner of the account is refused with `IDENTITY_LINK_CONFLICT`; a different account of the same installation with `IDENTITY_PROVIDER_ALREADY_LINKED`.
- **Link invitations.** An owner issues a 72-hour single-use invitation for a Person and provider (`/identidade/vincular#<secret>`). The person signs in to that provider (`intent: "invite"`); the proven account is attached to the invited Person. If the account had already created its own Person with nothing else in it, that Person is consolidated into the invited one.
- **Consolidation** (by proof or by an owner) runs in one transaction: links move, the WhatsApp number moves when the target has none (otherwise the source's is removed), the local credential and recovery codes move when the target has none (otherwise they are removed), grants are united (an unrestricted grant wins; two restricted grants are united only when they restrict the same dimensions and differ in at most one, otherwise the surviving Person's grant is kept, never widened), group memberships move, the agent module moves conversations through the `transferOwnership` port supplied at composition (refused with `IDENTITY_MERGE_BUSY` while a turn runs), the source's sessions end and it becomes `merged`. Owner roles are never absorbed. Access tokens already issued to the source remain valid for other modules until they expire (10 minutes).
- Unlinking requires recent authentication and never removes the last sign-in method. Owners may unlink for others under the same rule. Every link change is audited.
- Recent authentication is restored with the local password (+TOTP) (`POST /identity/me/reauthenticate`), a provider proof with `intent: "reauth"` (only for an account already linked to the Person), or a new embedded handshake.

## Authorization

[ADR-0037](../adr/0037-grant-identified-per-source-permissions-to-people-and-groups-with-data-restrictions.md) defines the model. Effective access is computed by the identity module from trusted state only.

### Catalog

Each grantable permission reads one data area of one source and has a stable code that people give support. The owning module declares it, with the dimensions by which it can be restricted; composition (`apps/api/src/modules.ts`) lists them.

| Code       | Permission                                    | Source                     | Restriction dimensions (code)                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| SNK-VEN-01 | `sales_sankhya:read` · Consultar vendas       | MNS (Sankhya)              | Empresa (EMP), Projeto/setor do item (PRJ, with subprojects), Vendedor (VEN, 0 = no seller), Grupo de produto (GRP, with subgroups), Centro de resultado (CR, with the centers below; awaits [PH-36](../project/human-actions.md#ph-36)) |
| VRM-VEN-01 | `sales_vrmaster:read` · Consultar vendas      | Pilar da Terra (VR Master) | Loja (LOJ), Comprador da venda (COM), Centro de custo da seção (CC, with the centers below), Mercadológico (MER, any level with the levels below)                                                                                        |
| SNK-PRD-01 | `products_sankhya:read` · Consultar produtos  | MNS (Sankhya)              | none: the registration is shared                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| VRM-PRD-01 | `products_vrmaster:read` · Consultar produtos | Pilar da Terra (VR Master) | none: the registration is shared                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| IAM-ADM-01 | `identity:admin` · Administração              | —                          | owners only, never granted                                                                                                                                                                                                               |

`sales:read` and `products:read` are capability permissions: identity adds them to the scopes of anyone holding a source permission of the family, and they are never granted. The [sales domain](sales-chat.md#access-and-restrictions) describes how each restriction is enforced.

### Grants, groups and restrictions

- A **grant** gives one permission to a Person or to a **group**. Owners manage groups in **Administração → Grupos**: name (unique regardless of case), optional description, members (active Persons) and grants. Every member holds the group's grants.
- A grant may be **restricted**. Within a grant, the values of one dimension are alternatives and every restricted dimension must match; a grant without restrictions admits every row of its source. Owners pick values with **Conceder com restrições** / **Editar restrições**, searching real records by code or name (`GET /identity/admin/restriction-values`); identity reads every submitted value back from the source before saving and stores its label. Unknown dimensions or values are `IDENTITY_RESTRICTION_INVALID`; an unreadable source is `IDENTITY_RESTRICTION_UNAVAILABLE`.
- A Person's scope for a permission is the **union** of its own grant and its groups' grants; any unrestricted grant admits everything. A grant restricted by a dimension its permission no longer declares cannot be enforced and is ignored.
- **Default user groups by provider:** owners select an existing group for PDT Connect and another for Sankhya (or no group) in **Administração → Parâmetros → Grupo de Usuário padrão por vínculo**. After verified sign-in through that provider, new and existing active people join the selected group before receiving their token. Its normal permissions and restrictions apply; links alone never grant permissions. Changes affect future sign-ins only. Memberships remain when the default changes, is disabled or the link is removed; owners manage them in Grupos. Manual removal is effective immediately, but the next sign-in through that provider rejoins its current default. Local login, token refresh, directory association and WhatsApp do not trigger membership. Selected groups cannot be deleted until their selection is cleared or replaced ([ADR-0038](../adr/0038-join-default-user-groups-after-verified-provider-sign-in.md)).
- **Owners** hold every catalog permission without restriction, plus `identity:admin`.

Grant, group and membership changes are audited (`grant.added`, `grant.updated`, `grant.removed`, `group.created`, `group.updated`, `group.deleted`, `group.member_added`, `group.member_removed`, `group.grant_added`, `group.grant_updated`, `group.grant_removed`) in the same transaction, with permission names and counts of restricted dimensions and values, never labels. Person detail and **Minha conta** show each effective permission with its code, its scope and where it comes from (owner, personal grant, group or provider link).

### Enforcement

Access tokens carry the permission strings (source and capability permissions). Modules that read data obtain the current permissions and scopes from identity at each request through a composition port (`accessSnapshot`): the agent, the legacy `/sales/chat` route and, per message, the WhatsApp channel. A revoked or narrowed grant therefore applies to the next request rather than at token expiry, and a principal that no longer designates an active Person is refused. Nothing from the browser, the host or the language model adds a permission or widens a scope.

When a request needs a permission the person does not hold, the agent answers instead of executing it, with a fixed message that lists each missing permission's code, title and source and asks the person to give the codes to support. Restricted results say they are limited to the person's access and are not the source's total; answers already in a conversation are shown and reused only while the reader's current scope covers the scope they were computed under ([corporate agent](corporate-agent.md)). Creating a profile from PDT grants no business capability until an owner grants one.

## Principal administrator, bootstrap and recovery

- `pnpm identity:bootstrap` (server shell, runtime configuration) prints a single-use 30-minute URL `/identidade/inicial#<secret>` when no active owner exists; `--break-glass` issues one even if owners exist. Both are audited. The page creates the owner with a local account and then requires TOTP before administration.
- Administration (`/admin`, `/identity/admin/*`) requires the owner role, a TOTP-assured session (unless a non-production policy makes the second factor optional), and recent authentication for changes (policy window, default 30 minutes). The last active owner cannot be removed or disabled, and owners cannot disable their own TOTP.
- Owners create Persons with any combination of: a 72-hour single-use local enrollment invitation (`/identidade/convite#enrollment=<secret>`); a Sankhya user selected from the ERP directory; and link invitations for PDT Connect or Sankhya. Associations always point to a real account: Sankhya users are re-read from the directory on the server (missing or expired-access accounts and accounts already linked elsewhere are refused before anything is created), and PDT accounts require the person's proof because PDT exposes no user directory to IA-MNS. The same actions exist for existing Persons, together with a new enrollment invitation and the reset invitation, which removes the local credential and revokes sessions. Invitation secrets travel in URL fragments, which are not sent to servers, and are removed from history on load.
- Owners consolidate two Persons of the same individual (`POST /identity/admin/persons/{personId}/merge`) when they cannot be proven together; owners are never the absorbed Person and overlapping providers are refused.
- The Sankhya directory (`GET /identity/admin/sankhya-users`) reads the DBA-provided view named in `SANKHYA_DIRECTORY_VIEW` (columns `CODUSU`, `NOMEUSU`, `NOMEUSUCPLT`, `EMAIL`, `DTLIMACESSO`) in a read-only transaction with its own two-connection pool and a 10-second call timeout, returning at most 20 users. It is off while the view is not configured ([PH-08](../project/human-actions.md#ph-08)). It needs only `SANKHYA_IDENTITY_ISSUER`, not the Om session connector, so it is not gated by PH-11.

## WhatsApp numbers

Owners link at most one WhatsApp number to a Person in **Administração → Pessoas → WhatsApp** (`PUT`/`DELETE /identity/admin/persons/{personId}/whatsapp`), with the same owner, second-factor and recent-confirmation checks as the rest of administration ([ADR-0036](../adr/0036-add-whatsapp-as-an-agent-channel-authorized-by-owner-linked-numbers.md)). Nobody can link, change or remove their own number in any other way, and there is no self-service through WhatsApp; **Minha conta** only shows the number.

- **Entry.** International form with country code (`+55 11 98765-4321` or `0055…`); spaces, dots, dashes and parentheses are ignored. National numbers are refused (`IDENTITY_WHATSAPP_NUMBER_INVALID`), and a Brazilian mobile without its ninth digit is refused with its own message (`IDENTITY_WHATSAPP_NINTH_DIGIT_REQUIRED`). The number is stored as E.164 digits in `identity_whatsapp_links`.
- **Uniqueness.** A number belongs to at most one Person, and so does its deterministic legacy Brazilian form, which WhatsApp may report for older mobile accounts ([identifiers](whatsapp-channel.md#identifiers)); a conflict is `IDENTITY_WHATSAPP_NUMBER_IN_USE`. Only active Persons receive a number.
- **Audit.** `whatsapp.linked`, `whatsapp.changed` and `whatsapp.removed` record the owner, the affected Person, the time and only the last four digits, in the same transaction as the change; `whatsapp.confirmed` records the first accepted message.
- **Generation.** The Person retains the last allocated WhatsApp link version even after removal. Creation, number changes and transfers allocate a later version under the Person lock in the same transaction as the audit. Recreating a number never reauthorizes work queued for a former link.
- **Revocation.** Removing or changing the number, disabling the Person or consolidating it takes effect for the next message and for every reply not yet handed to Meta, because the channel asks identity again before processing and before sending. A changed number forgets what was learned about the previous one.
- **Authorization.** For each signature-verified message, identity returns exactly one active Person and that Person's effective permissions computed now, as for a new access token; the number grants nothing by itself. Merging moves the absorbed Person's number to the surviving one when it has none; otherwise the absorbed number is removed.

A linked phone works as a credential without a second factor; see the channel's [residual risks](whatsapp-channel.md#security-notes-and-residual-risks).

## Authentication policy

Owners manage it at `/admin` → **Autenticação e segurança** (`GET`/`PUT /identity/admin/security-policy`). It is one row in `identity_security_policies`; without it the defaults apply.

| Setting (screen label)                                            | Default           | Allowed                                            | Reported as reducing security above |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Duração máxima de uma sessão (`sessionMaxMinutes`)                | 12 h              | 1 h – 30 days                                      | 24 h                                |
| Encerrar a sessão após inatividade (`idleTimeoutMinutes`)         | 2 h               | 15 min – 7 days, not longer than the session       | 8 h                                 |
| Confirmação para alterar a própria conta (`recentAuthMinutes`)    | 10 min            | 5 min – 24 h, not longer than the session          | 1 h                                 |
| Confirmação para ações administrativas (`adminRecentAuthMinutes`) | 30 min            | 5 min – 4 h                                        | 1 h                                 |
| Verificação em duas etapas (`mfaRequirement`)                     | administrators    | everyone, administrators, none (not in production) | none                                |
| Pedir o código de verificação novamente (`rememberDeviceDays`)    | every sign-in (0) | 0 – 90 days                                        | 30 days                             |

- **Duration and inactivity.** A direct session ends at sign-in + duration, whatever the use, or when no activity is reported for the inactivity period. The browser reports activity (clicks, typing, scrolling, returning to the tab) with each renewal of its 10-minute access token; renewals of an untouched tab keep the token fresh but do not postpone inactivity. Renewals are serialized across tabs, a tab that returns from sleep renews at once, and a renewal presenting the credential replaced in the last 30 seconds is a concurrent renewal that receives a token without a new credential; later reuse revokes the session. When a session ends, the person returns to `/entrar` with "Sua sessão terminou".
- **Recent authentication.** Changes to one's own password, links or second factor need a sign-in or confirmation within the account window; administrative changes within the administrative window. Confirmation uses the password and code, a provider proof, or a new embedded handshake.
- **Second factor.** It applies to sign-in with an IA-MNS password (PDT Connect and Sankhya sign-ins are protected by those systems). With `everyone` every such account, and with `administrators` every owner, must use it: an account without it gets `mfa_enrollment_required` after the password (also right after setting a password from an enrollment or reset invitation) and sets up the authenticator before any session opens (`POST /identity/login/mfa-enrollment` and `/confirm`), and cannot remove the factor while required. The code is asked at every sign-in, unless the person chose to remember the browser (offered only when `rememberDeviceDays` > 0, never inside a host page, and to owners only while the requirement is `none`, which production never allows). A remembered browser keeps an HttpOnly `ia-mns-device` cookie whose token is stored hashed in `identity_trusted_devices`; it skips the code but yields an ordinary (not second-factor) session. Password changes, removing the factor, an owner reset or **Voltar a pedir o código em todos** in Minha conta forget remembered browsers.
- **Changes.** Saving requires an owner session with the second factor (per policy) and recent administrative confirmation; values outside the limits are refused (`IDENTITY_POLICY_INVALID`), `none` in production is refused (`IDENTITY_POLICY_NOT_ALLOWED`), and newly reduced settings require `acknowledgeReducedSecurity` (`IDENTITY_POLICY_CONFIRMATION_REQUIRED`). Each change is audited (`policy.updated`) with previous and new values and listed on the screen. Open direct sessions are re-derived at once from their sign-in and last activity (shorter values may end them, including the editor's); second-factor rules apply at the next sign-in, while administration always checks the current rule. Other API instances follow within 15 seconds.
- **Not configurable.** Production always requires the second factor for administrators; outside a non-production `none` policy, owners never skip the code through a remembered browser; access tokens stay 10 minutes; embedded sessions renew silently every 10 minutes; ticket, flow and invitation lifetimes, lockout, refresh rotation with reuse detection, rate limits and audit are fixed.

## Operational parameters

Owners administer the [operational parameters](../architecture/configuration.md#operational-parameters) at `/admin` → **Parâmetros** (`GET /identity/admin/parameters`, `PUT /identity/admin/parameters/{key}`, [ADR-0028](../adr/0028-administer-operational-parameters-separately-from-secrets-and-bootstrap-configuration.md)): the AI model, the AI trace level and the WhatsApp channel switch. Identity hosts this administration because it owns the principal administrator; the parameters themselves are declared by the runtime catalog and read by their owning consumers. Default provider groups have separate identity-owned storage and endpoints (`GET /identity/admin/provider-groups`, `PUT /identity/admin/provider-groups/{provider}`), despite appearing on the same page.

- **Who.** The same check as all administration: the owner role, a second-factor session (per the authentication policy), and recent administrative confirmation for changes. Anyone else receives `IDENTITY_ACCESS_DENIED` for listing, reading and changing; hiding the tab is not the protection.
- **What is shown.** Each parameter's value in use, the installation default, whether an owner value or the default applies, who changed it and when, when a change takes effect, and the last 20 changes. Environment variable names, secrets and bootstrap settings are never part of the response, and unknown keys (including secret names) are `IDENTITY_PARAMETER_NOT_FOUND`.
- **Changes.** A change applies only to the version the owner read (`IDENTITY_PARAMETER_CONFLICT` otherwise); values outside the parameter's domain are `IDENTITY_PARAMETER_INVALID`, values this environment refuses (content tracing in production) are `IDENTITY_PARAMETER_NOT_ALLOWED`, and `null` restores the installation default. Each change is audited as `parameter.updated` with the parameter, previous and new effective values (lists comma-separated), whether it was a reset and the new version, in the same database transaction as the change, so a change never persists without its audit event; parameters never hold secrets. The installation default `OPENAI_MODEL` must satisfy the same identifier domain as `ai.model`, so the default is always a value an owner could also save.
- **Effect.** No restart: the AI model applies to the next AI request, the trace level to the next turn, and the WhatsApp switch to the next message. Default group selection applies on the next verified provider sign-in, without a restart.

## Security properties

- Secrets (tickets, refresh credentials, recovery codes, assertion ids) are 256-bit random values or high-entropy codes stored only as SHA-256. Passwords are scrypt verifiers; TOTP secrets are AES-256-GCM sealed. None are logged or returned after creation.
- Failures for unknown logins and wrong passwords are identical, and a dummy hash equalizes timing. Lockout is temporary (from the 5th failure: 1, 2, 4… up to 60 minutes). Login, verification and flow endpoints are rate limited.
- Cookie-authenticated endpoints (refresh, provisioning and consolidation by cookie, signed-in detection at login start) accept only same-origin web requests (`x-ia-mns-client: web`, `Sec-Fetch-Site`, `Origin`). A consolidation ticket is bound to the session that proved both profiles.
- Audit (`identity_audit_events`) is append-only for the runtime role and stores only allowlisted codes.
- All identity responses are `Cache-Control: no-store`.

## Deployment requirements

- HTTPS `IA_MNS_PUBLIC_ORIGIN`; the signing and encryption keys in the secret store, generated for each environment with `pnpm identity:keys --output <new file>` (or `node dist/cli/identity-keys.js` beside a build), which writes an owner-only file and prints no key material. Never reuse local keys.
- Serve the web and the API under the same origin: the API process serves the web build (`ORION_WEB_ROOT`) and answers under `/api`, the browser path of `PDT_IDENTITY_REDIRECT_URI` (`https://<ia-mns>/api/identity/pdt/callback`) and of the Sankhya callback (`https://<ia-mns>/api/identity/sankhya/callback`) ([ADR-0029](../adr/0029-serve-the-web-and-api-from-one-origin-behind-restricted-trusted-proxies.md)).
- Every response sends `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, which keeps the real `Origin` on the web's `fetch()` writes (a native form POST would carry `Origin: null` and fail the cookie endpoints' check), and `Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors 'none'` except `/embed/pdt` and `/embed/sankhya`, which allow only `PDT_EMBED_ORIGIN` and `SANKHYA_EMBED_ORIGIN` respectively; the serving API derives this from the identity configuration. The development and preview servers reuse the provider origins from the root ignored `.env.local`, with shell values taking precedence. An explicit `ORION_WEB_EMBED_ANCESTORS` overrides both surfaces; an empty value disables framing.
- Behind the TLS reverse proxy, `ORION_TRUSTED_PROXIES` lists only that proxy, so login and verification limits apply per client; issuer, redirects and cookie security still come from `IA_MNS_PUBLIC_ORIGIN`, never from forwarded headers.
- The first owner comes from `pnpm identity:bootstrap`, or `node dist/cli/identity-bootstrap.js` beside a build, run once with the runtime configuration.
- Configuration names are listed in the [generated configuration reference](../generated/configuration/api.md).

## Limits and next steps

- The embedded Sankhya integration passed in the Sankhya development Om only; the direct-URL Sankhya sign-in page is pending ([PJ-25](../project/implementation-plan.md#current-work)), and production needs PH-11, PH-14 and PH-17. The embedded PDT integration passed with a local PDT Connect ([PJ-27](../project/implementation-plan.md#current-work)); HTTPS homologation and production are PH-12. Development, synthetic and proof-of-concept results are not production evidence.
- Passkeys, e-mail recovery, SCIM or automated deprovisioning from Sankhya/PDT, Sankhya-group-to-permission mappings, explicit denials and restrictions of product lookup are not implemented. Deactivating a user in Sankhya or PDT stops new proofs; existing IA-MNS sessions end at their expiry or on owner action.
- PDT authorization data (permissions and stores) is not yet mapped to IA-MNS capabilities; future PDT capabilities must re-authorize with PDT at execution time, as the contract requires.
- Retention of identity data, audit and sessions follows the shared-deployment policy still pending in [PH-09](../project/human-actions.md#ph-09).

## Replacing the former provider permission policy

Migration `202610210001_identity_provider_groups` starts both provider selections empty. Before shared use, create or choose the desired groups, review their permissions and restrictions, then select them in Parâmetros ([PH-13](../project/human-actions.md#ph-13)). Existing explicit grants and memberships remain. The former `access.providerGrants` parameter and `IA_MNS_PROVIDER_GRANTS` environment variable no longer grant access; historical rows and audit remain for evidence. A broader group grant is united with personal grants, so a restricted personal grant cannot narrow an unrestricted default group.
